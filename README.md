# OUR TANK

Four friends, one living aquarium. Chunky voxel fish and decorations, modern lighting, and a calm loop built for short visits. Runs in any phone browser and installs to the home screen (it is a web app, not an App Store binary).

## The game

- **Start:** an empty tank where you choose the colour of your octopus (six colours), name it, and a starter pack of free items. Feed it, meet your friends, place something. Four short tips, skippable.
- **Care:** the Care tab opens with the one thing that matters most right now ("Inky is hungry. Feed Inky a crab."), and a tap does it; feed (tap the water to drop food, three drops per feeding; an octopus is fed crabs, which it hunts, while flakes and pellets are for the fish, and a hungry octopus's card has its own "Feed Inky a crab" button; there is no separate paid crab treat any more, a real meal counts as a crab caught), wipe algae off the glass, change the water. You earn **shells** only when the tank actually needs the care. No streaks, no timers you must obey, no ads, no purchases.
- **The rhythm of care:** feeding is the everyday visit, about twice a day (a meal lasts the fish about 12 hours, a crab lasts an octopus about 12 hours), because watching them eat is the fun part. The water and the glass are a bigger job every three days or so, and pay more for it (+6 and +4, a pearl every fifth wipe +6). Every meter and tile in Care says when its thing is next due ("Next meal in about 9 hours", "Due for a change in about 2 days"). The timings live in one table, `CARE` in `web/src/game/rules.js`. `node tools/econ.mjs` plays simulated players through the rules and reports chores per day and what they pay: at three visits a day this rhythm is about four chores a day instead of nine and a half, and a once-a-day player earns the same shells as before.
- **Selling a fish:** Crew, then Collection, lists every fish in the tank, your octopus first. Tap one to sell it to a new home: it leaves the tank and the shared wallet gets half of what it cost (a fish that came free, such as the first fish or a hatched egg, fetches nothing, so it cannot be farmed). Only a fish's own caretaker can sell it, except that the tank's first caretaker (whoever sits in the first seat) can sell anyone's, in case someone stops playing. The last fish in the tank is never sold. `node tools/sell_check.mjs` checks it in the browser.
- **Grow:** adopt fish (goldfish, neon school, guppies, corydoras, blue ram, angelfish, betta) and place decorations (18 items: plants, kelp, rocks, driftwood, lantern, chest, bubbler, clam, skull, torii gate, stone arch…). Fish grow baby → juvenile → adult over real days and the tank levels up (8 levels), unlocking new species, items and room.
- **Every fish has its own needs:** it gets hungry at its own pace (greedy fish sooner, lazy fish later), its happiness depends on clean water and on things it likes in the tank (shy fish like plants, curious fish like structures, playful fish like bubbles…), and its health slips if it is neglected. Tap a fish to see its mood.
- **Neglect has a cost.** A fish that goes a day and a half with no food at all, or sits in water left unchanged for about five days, slowly weakens. After two days it turns pale, slows down and a warning goes out; after five days it dies and floats belly-up at the surface until someone taps it to lay it to rest (a floating fish also fouls the water faster). Any real care wins the time back twice as fast. Guard rails keep it fair in a shared tank: no deaths in a tank's first three days, at most one death a day, and the last fish never dies. Lost fish are listed under Remembered in the Journal.
- **Playing with a fish:** tap a fish, then Play, and drag your finger along the glass. It follows. Five seconds builds your bond with it.
- **Together:** up to **four** caretakers share one tank with a six-character code or invite link. Everything is shared: fish, decorations, shells, journal, chat. The Crew tab shows who is in the tank and one "What's happening" feed, newest first, where messages and what everyone did sit together by day. The tank's name, code, invites and a new code live in Settings.
- **Things that bring you back, without streaks:** gifts turn up in the tank (an octopus, a fish or the filter gets the blame) and wait for you forever; new fish arrive after a short wait; one fish a day is a quarter cheaper; rare visitors (Moon Betta, Sun Angelfish, Rose Corydoras) drop by for a few hours and are added to the collection book when you say hello; two adult fish of one species sometimes lay an egg that hatches into a blend of both; the journal tells small true stories about your fish; friends can wash a message in a bottle into the tank for each other; the tank has a birthday every week.
- **Personalities are behaviour:** shy fish hide behind plants and bolt when others come close, brave fish swim to the glass, curious fish inspect decorations, social fish stick to a buddy, playful fish chase bubbles, lazy fish rest low, greedy fish wait by the surface. After dark most fish drift low and slow. Every fish is a little different in colour and size.
- **An octopus is worth watching:** left to itself it does impressions, each with the creature's colours and skin as well as its shape (a banded sole, cream with chocolate bands, flat on the sand; a maroon-and-cream striped lionfish with spiky skin, up in the water; a sea snake, its body hidden in the sand and two black-and-white ringed arms out; a milky lilac jellyfish, bell up, arms trailing; a plant in that plant's own greens or purples, swaying with it; and any fish it tags along behind, whose colours and markings it copies, so behind a clownfish it turns orange with white bands), wraps its arms round plants, rocks and its shell and pulls itself up a plant's stem, digs into the sand until only its eyes show, tags along behind a fish, sits up and watches one, swims a circuit of the tank, and walks in four different ways. A fish that hangs about too close gets smacked with an arm, or has silt, a squirt of water or a shell from its hoard thrown at it; a quick one close by makes it jump (sometimes with ink). It picks its shell up and carries it somewhere new, turns it, and tucks in under it. Its card says what it is up to. `node tools/octopus_acts.mjs` forces each act, then leaves it alone for eight minutes of tank time to prove it does these things by itself; `node tools/octopus_soak.mjs 20` leaves it in a realistic tank for twenty minutes and counts everything it did; `OUT=dir node tools/shots_acts.mjs` photographs each impression from the front, side and top, and `node tools/shots_grab.mjs` checks it takes hold of a plant, a rock and its shell and smacks a fish. In a shared tank only the lead phone decides what it does next; the others are shown it.
- **Each octopus has a character you can see:** its kind (explorer, homebody, show-off, watcher, collector, trickster) decides what it likes doing (an explorer roams and handles things, a homebody buries itself and plays plant, a show-off does the lionfish and the jellyfish, more so with an audience, a watcher watches, a trickster squirts water at the glass at whoever is watching), how brisk it is, and how it sits (a bold one up tall, a timid one low). Each has a favourite trick, named on its card. Before it acts it pauses and looks at what it has in mind; it draws a breath before each jet stroke; it squints at a rival or in a temper and goes wide-eyed when startled; now and then it turns its eyes on you. It remembers the fish it likes following (its best friend) and the one that keeps startling it (its rival, which it watches narrowly and chases off sooner); both are kept with the tank, so every phone's card names the same fish. Its memorable moments (each impression, copying a fish, a smack, a squirt at you, a new friend or grudge) are written in the journal, each kind at most once a day or so. In disguise even the rings of its eyes change colour. `node tools/octopus_character.mjs` checks all of this.
- **An octopus knows its way round the floor:** it feels ahead and to both sides and goes round rocks, shells and plant stems (fish still swim through the leaves), keeps to one side of a thing rather than dithering, picks a clear spot before it sets off, and when it is boxed in on foot it jets over the top and carries on from where it lands. Its arms only walk when its body is actually getting somewhere. `node tools/octopus_obstacles.mjs` rehearses seven trips across a cluttered floor.
- **Make it yours:** pick the floor (sand, pearl, gravel, black sand, pink coral) and the backdrop. The light follows your phone's clock.
- **While you are away** the tank keeps living: hunger rises, water clouds, algae grows, fish grow. It is capped so a holiday never hurts the fish.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm start          # http://localhost:8080 – game + shared-tank server
npm test           # 6 rule tests + 35 server tests
```

Without the server (static hosting) the game runs solo and saves to the browser.
Handy URL options: `?fps=1` frame-rate meter (with the time the simulation takes per frame) · `?q=0|1|2` pin graphics quality · `?tod=night` time of day · `?dev=1` test tools in Settings (+50 shells, skip a day).

## Deploy (so friends can join over the internet)

You need any host that runs a Docker container with a small persistent disk. HTTPS is required for invite links, sharing and the home-screen install.

- **Render:** New → Blueprint → select this repo (`render.yaml` is included).
- **Fly.io:** `fly launch --copy-config --no-deploy && fly volumes create ourtank_data --size 1 && fly deploy` (`fly.toml` is included).
- **Anything else:** `docker build -t our-tank . && docker run -p 8080:8080 -v ourtank:/data our-tank`.

Environment: `PORT` (default 8080), `DB` (SQLite path, default `ourtank.db`). `DEV=1` allows the developer actions; never set it in production. One instance only (SQLite + in-memory live rooms).

## Layout

- `web/` the client, no build step. `src/main.js` wiring · `src/game/` rules + game state (shared with the server) · `src/w3/` 3D stage, fish, items, plants · `src/ui.js` interface · `src/online.js` onboarding + realtime.
- `server/` Node + SQLite + WebSocket. The server runs the same `rules.js` as the browser and is authoritative for membership, shells, purchases and progression.
- `tools/` screenshot helpers, a four-browser end-to-end test (`e2e.mjs`) and a full solo playthrough (`playthrough.mjs`).

## Rules the server enforces

- Exactly four seats per tank (`CHECK slot 1..4` + `UNIQUE(tank, slot)`, claimed inside one write transaction). A fifth player gets "This tank is full". A database made when tanks held three is rebuilt to four seats the first time the new server opens it.
- Codes: 6 characters from `23456789ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ`, random, unique, regenerable by any member. A code only lets you ask for a seat; attempts are rate limited.
- Every action carries an idempotency key, so a retry or a double tap never pays or charges twice. Two players spending the last shells at the same time: exactly one purchase succeeds.
- Identity is an anonymous account token kept in the browser (only its hash is stored).

## Keeping tanks across redeploys (do one of these)

A host whose disk is wiped on every deploy or restart (Render's free plan) forgets every tank, and the codes stop working. Pick one:

**A. An offsite copy (free).** The server copies its whole database to a bucket every five minutes and just before it stops, and puts it back when it starts with nothing. Any S3-compatible bucket works. With Backblaze B2 (free 10 GB, no card needed):
1. Create a Backblaze account, then Buckets, Create a Bucket (keep it private), for example `our-tank-data`.
2. App Keys, Add a New Application Key, limited to that bucket with read and write. Copy the keyID and the applicationKey now; the key is shown once.
3. On the bucket page note its Endpoint, like `s3.us-west-004.backblazeb2.com`. The region is the middle part, `us-west-004`.
4. In Render, open the service, Environment, and add: `S3_ENDPOINT` = `https://s3.us-west-004.backblazeb2.com`, `S3_BUCKET` = your bucket name, `S3_KEY` = the keyID, `S3_SECRET` = the applicationKey, `S3_REGION` = `us-west-004`. Save, and let it redeploy.
5. Open `/admin`: "Offsite copy" should say on, with the time of the last copy. Cloudflare R2 and Supabase Storage work the same way.

**A2. The same, kept in your own GitHub (no new account).**
1. On GitHub create a new **private** repository, for example `ourtank-data`, and tick "Add a README file".
2. Settings, Developer settings, Personal access tokens, Fine-grained tokens, Generate new token. Name it `our-tank`, choose the longest expiry, Repository access: Only select repositories, pick `ourtank-data`. Under Permissions, Repository permissions, set **Contents** to Read and write. Generate and copy the token (shown once). Put a reminder in your calendar to make a new one before it expires.
3. In Render, Environment: `GITHUB_BACKUP_TOKEN` = the token, `GITHUB_BACKUP_REPO` = `yourname/ourtank-data`. Save.
4. `/admin` shows "Offsite copy: on". Copies are compressed and sent every 25 minutes while something changed, always when the server stops, and within about ten seconds of a moment people would really miss (a tank made or joined, an octopus chosen, a fish bought), at most once a minute; Settings, "Tank storage" shows Protected once the first copy is sent. It stores all accounts and tanks in that private repo.
5. Redeploys are safe: Render starts the new server before it stops the old one, so a new server sends nothing for its first three minutes, and if the old server's last copy arrives meanwhile it loads that copy instead of overwriting it. `node tools/backup_drill.mjs` rehearses a wiped-disk restart and an overlapping redeploy against a stand-in for GitHub.

**B. A persistent disk.** On a paid Render plan, mount a disk at `/data` and set `DB=/data/ourtank.db` (the included `render.yaml` does this).

Phones also keep a copy of their tank and put it back under the same code if the server ever loses it anyway. When several phones share one tank, the first to open the app puts it back and the others join that one (the server refuses to make a second copy under a code that is in use), and whatever only a later phone's copy knew is folded in: fish, decorations and orders it has are added, the copy saved later wins where both differ, and shells and level never go down; each phone sends the ids it used to have, so everyone gets their own octopus and gifts back. `node tools/forget.mjs` rehearses this in two real browsers. If an octopus is ever left with no caretaker in the crew, its card offers "Make it mine" (to someone without one) and "Let it go".

## Notifications

What they say, when nobody has the tank open: a fish has died, a fish is in a critical state, a rare visitor, a solved puzzle jar, an arrival or a hatch, a growth milestone or a discovery. When nothing happened but care is overdue: your own octopus is hungry (only you are told), the fish are hungry or the water needs changing (everyone), each at most once in 12 hours. Also: a friend joined your tank, a nudge, a bottle. At most one a day per person (two when a friend writes to you), never between 22:00 and 08:00 local time.

They go to the caretakers who do not have the tank open at that moment, including when a friend does. News (an arrival, a death, a visitor, a solved jar, a milestone) can come twice a day; nudges and care reminders once.

**The server has to be awake to send them.** On Render's free plan the service goes to sleep after fifteen minutes with no visitors and nothing runs until someone opens the app, so a fish that arrives while everyone is away is only noticed, and announced, when the next person opens the tank (and they see it themselves). To get notifications while nobody is playing, keep the server awake with a free uptime pinger (UptimeRobot, cron-job.org, Better Stack): have it fetch `https://YOUR-APP.onrender.com/healthz` every 5 to 10 minutes. A paid plan that does not sleep needs nothing.

They work with no setup: the server makes its own key pair the first time it starts and keeps it in the database (set `VAPID_PUBLIC`, `VAPID_PRIVATE` and `VAPID_SUBJECT` yourself only if you want to bring your own). Keep the disk attached, or phones will have to switch notifications back on after a redeploy. Players get a Notifications switch in Settings, plus a "Send a test" button to check that they arrive. At most two a day, never between 22:00 and 08:00 their time, never a "come back" reminder.

On iPhone they only work from the home-screen icon: in Safari tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then open Our Tank from that icon (remove an older icon first) and switch them on in Settings. iOS 16.4 or later. The phone tells the server its subscription again every time the app opens and whenever the browser renews it, so nudges keep coming after a reinstall, a renewal or a tank put back under new accounts.

## Usage tracking and the developer view

The server records anonymous events (a random player id, a random tank id, an event name and a number; no names, no IP addresses, no message text): sessions (counting only time the game is visible), care actions, fish played with and inspected, decorations placed, shells earned and spent, daily wishes completed, discoveries, journal opened, friend interactions, fish deaths and level-ups. Set `ADMIN_KEY` on the server, then open `/admin?key=YOUR_KEY` (or `/admin/stats?key=YOUR_KEY` for JSON). It shows individual-player numbers (daily active players, session length, visits per day, retention on days 1, 7 and 30, what a session contains) separately from shared-tank numbers (active tanks, caretakers per tank, level distribution, deaths). Three people opening one tank count as three players and one tank. Without `ADMIN_KEY` the page does not exist.

## Known limits

- No App Store build. iPhone haptics are not available to web apps (Android vibrates).
- Recovery: every account has a recovery key (shown when you create a tank, and under Settings). Typing it on a new phone signs you back in and retires the old phone's token. Without the key, clearing browser data loses the identity.
- One server instance. The database keeps a rolling backup next to it (`ourtank.db.backup`, every six hours and on shutdown).

## If a tank or recovery key "disappears"
That means the server started with an empty database. On Render the database must live on the persistent disk: the service needs a disk mounted at `/data` and the environment variable `DB=/data/ourtank.db` (the blueprint in `render.yaml` does both, and needs a paid plan). Without a disk every deploy or restart wipes all tanks and recovery keys. The server log prints the database path and the number of players it found at start, and warns when it is not on `/data`; the developer page (`/admin`) shows "Data since", which should not reset after a deploy.

## Your data
Settings has **Backup of this tank** (downloads a JSON file with no account ids), **Delete my data** (removes the player; an empty tank goes with its last player) and a link to `/privacy.html`. On the welcome screen, **Restore a backup** makes a new tank from a backup file (validated and bounded). The server endpoints are `GET /api/export`, `POST /api/import` and `DELETE /api/me`.

## Looks cost a few shells
Sand and the Candy backdrop are free; other floors (20 to 40) and backdrops (25) are bought once with shells and kept by the tank. A tank that already uses a look keeps it.

## Quiet surprises
Nothing here pays shells or writes to the journal. Every few minutes, once the tutorial is done, something passes through the deep water behind the tank (a whale shark, a jellyfish, a turtle or a silver shoal, drawn as pale ghosts: the fish remembering the ocean), and holding a finger in the water calls the bold and curious fish over to look. `tools/shots_sights.mjs` parks each sighting mid-water for screenshots.

## Look
The far water has two layers of hazy, swaying kelp blades and rock outcrops behind the dunes (`buildBackdrop` in `web/src/w3/env.js`, tinted by the chosen backdrop and time of day). The menus share one skin: pixel-notched, water-tinted panels (the block at the end of the style sheet in `web/index.html`). `tools/shots_look.mjs` captures the tank at each time of day.

## The sand banks
The bottom of the tank is one continuous landscape of sand with three levels: a high bank at the back on the left, a middle terrace across the tank, and the open floor at the front. They are part of the tank, not decorations, and every tank has them (a new tank still starts with no decorations; the free starter pack is unchanged). The banks are sand, so their faces slope rather than stand as walls, and the levels are joined by two long, gentle ramps on the right: the back bank runs down to the terrace, and the terrace runs down to the floor.

- **One height for everything.** `web/src/w3/terrain.js` says how high the sand is anywhere (`sandH`), how steep it is, which level a spot is on, and where the ramps are. The sand mesh, the octopus, the fish, decorations, crabs, food, eggs and the finger choosing where to put something all ask it, so nothing floats or sinks. It is pure numbers and read from a table worked out once, so it costs almost nothing per frame.
- **The octopus uses the whole landscape.** It roams all three levels, follows the sand up and down, and to get from one level to another on foot it takes the ramps: a face too steep to walk counts as a wall. When the way round by the ramps is much longer than the way across, it usually swims instead (an octopus carrying something, hunting or busy at a job always walks). It sets off now and then just to explore the sand, more often in a tank with little else in it, and when it wanders it prefers a level the other octopuses are not on, so several spread out instead of huddling. Up on the high bank it likes to stay a while and look out. Shells and rocks it shoves along stay on their own level, a crab dropped for it lands on its own level, and an impression up in the water (the lionfish, the jellyfish) rises from the sand it is on.
- **How much each level is used.** Over twenty minutes with three octopuses and no decorations, the floor and the terrace each get roughly 40 to 50 per cent of their time and the high bank, the smallest and furthest level, roughly 4 to 12 per cent. That is left to them, not forced.
- **Framing.** The camera sits higher and looks down more steeply, so the top of the sand stays about halfway up the screen while the front floor runs down to just above the tab bar. The front glass (`GLASS_Z` in `terrain.js`) is well forward, so the open floor runs right down to the tab bar: an octopus walks all the way down there, and a decoration can be placed there too. When an octopus wanders onto the floor it mostly heads for the open sand down by the glass. Opened from the home screen, the app fills the whole screen, home-bar area included. An octopus on the front floor is at the bottom of the screen, one on the high bank near the middle. Roaming is a little more frequent and favours the high bank, and an octopus that gets up there lingers. `node tools/screen_spread.mjs` checks where everything spends its time on the screen and on each level (with a typical tank: front floor about 45 to 50 per cent, terrace 30 to 40, high bank 12 to 15).
- **Open water up top.** The fish that live in open water (Blue Chromis, Pink Anthias, Damselfish, Emperor Angelfish) keep to the upper half of the screen, wherever they are going: the halfway line sits lower in the water further back, and their floor follows it. Food still brings them down, and a sick fish can still sink. The lower half is left to the reef fish, the seahorses and the octopuses, so the tank does not feel crowded.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** An octopus's arms lie on the real sand: any point of an arm or its webbing that would be under the sand, on a slope or against a bank, is lifted onto it, and its body is drawn lifted clear of a slope beside it. Each arm is followed out from the body, and if it would meet a rock, a pot, the coconut shell or a plant it is drawn back to stop short (eased, so it never snaps), unless it is wrapped round something it is holding. Fish keep their nose, tail and flank above the sand, deep-bodied fish a little more, and they keep off plants. Plants lean gently away from an octopus or fish among them; each voxel of a leaf moves as one, so nothing stretches. A fish gets out of an octopus's way, never the other way round, so a passing fish cannot shove an octopus off a ramp. A seahorse picks a holdfast with room for its body and tail. `node tools/clip_check.mjs [minutes]` checks it voxel by voxel against the sand and every decoration; on purpose (burying, the sea-snake impression, a den, holding on) is counted apart.
- **Placing things.** A decoration goes onto the sand under your finger, on whichever level you touch, snapped to a 0.2 grid. A face is too steep to stand anything on, so a spot on one moves to the nearest flat sand. Decorations bed into the sand a little on a slope rather than float. Old tanks keep every decoration where it was: each one now rests on the sand at its spot.
- **Light.** The camera sits a little higher and looks down, so the tops of the banks catch the eye as well as their faces. The faces are a darker, browner sand with the layers it was laid down in, the hollow where a face meets the level below is in shade, and the lip of each ledge catches the light. Slow, broad sunbeams come down from the surface, and moving caustics are drawn on the sand itself, strongest on flat tops and faint on the faces.

`node tools/terrain_check.mjs` sends an octopus from the front floor to the top of the back bank and back (it must go by the ramps and the terrace, never straight up a face), leaves three octopuses alone for twenty minutes of tank time and checks they use every level and that no fish or octopus ever goes into the sand, and places a decoration with a finger on each level and on a face.

## Seahorses
A seahorse spends most of its time holding on to something with its tail, close to home. It is always in one state at a time, worked out on the lead phone in order of priority: social (greeting or holding tails), feeding (striking, or going after food), releasing, approaching, settling, night, watching, hitched, exploring, swimming. While anchored, each eye looks its own way. It slowly takes on a little of its holdfast's colour, and lets go to drift to another after a while. Two seahorses that have shared the tank a while now and then hold tails: they swim to meet, face each other, curl their tails together and drift as a pair for half a minute or so, then let go (food in the water breaks it up at once). That never happens on a newcomer's first day, and it happens more often the longer they have been together and the more sociable they are. There is no pairing, courtship or breeding. `node tools/seahorse_check.mjs OUTDIR` and `node tools/seahorse_phase1.mjs [section,...]` check it.

**Places it knows.** Each seahorse remembers the holdfasts it has tried, by decoration id, in its fish log on the server: visits, time held, when it was last there and where the decoration stood. So the memory survives a reload, a new caretaker and a change of lead phone. A favourite is earned: the holdfast it has held longest, once it has held it for two minutes or more over at least two visits. It is never handed out. A decoration that was moved is still the same place to it, though less certain for a while. One that was removed is dropped from its memory, and if it was the favourite, the seahorse lets it go.

**Personality.** Its curiosity, boldness, sociability, activity and adaptability (from its traits and its seed) weight its choices rather than fixing them. A curious seahorse tries new holdfasts and goes to look at a new decoration. A cautious one keeps to its favourite. A bold one holds on higher and nearer the glass. An active one moves on sooner. An adaptable one goes back to a moved decoration sooner. A sociable one settles near another seahorse and holds tails more.

**Something new.** When a decoration is placed, each seahorse may notice it after a moment of its own; the nearer and the more curious, the likelier. A curious one lets go and goes to look at it at its real height. One that stays holding on may just turn an eye to it.

**Feeding.** Its mouth is the tip of its snout. Holding on, it watches food drift past: its eyes lock on, it turns on its stem and tips its head to follow, and when a piece comes within reach of the snout (0.3 tank units) it strikes fast and sucks it in, without letting go. It switches to a piece that comes clearly nearer than the one it is watching. While food is still falling, or drifting near its own height, it keeps watching and its patience runs down only slowly; once nothing promising is left near it (a bold seahorse is less patient; a weak one never lets go for food; a resting one at night lets go only if the tank is really hungry), it may let go and go after food elsewhere.

**Currents and mysis.** The water is never quite still: a slow current, turning along the tank and back over a minute or so and a little different at each depth, carries drifting food (`current()` in `fishmgr.js`). Sinking flakes drift with it. Mysis sink to a depth of their own and then, like real mysids, keep near cover: each hangs about the nearest plant, in and out of its leaves at stem height, until the current carries it to another. Nothing is drawn towards a seahorse: a plant with no seahorse on it gets the same shrimp. Other fish leave mysis alone for its first eight seconds down among the plants, then come for what is left; no other fish takes a piece right in front of a seahorse that is watching it. Nothing in the economy changed: mysis is still the free food it was. `node tools/seahorse_feeding.mjs [feeds] [seed]` feeds an ordinary mixed tank the way a player does and counts how many seahorse meals are taken from the stem.

**Night.** At night each seahorse goes to rest at its own time, on a holdfast it knows (most often its favourite), holds on longer and moves its fins slowly. In the morning each comes back to its day at its own time. Hunger and illness still come first.

**Shared tanks.** Only the lead phone decides where a seahorse holds on, when it strikes, and whom it greets or holds tails with. With the other positions it sends a short state for each seahorse (state, eyes, colour, glow, strike count and the grip it holds), and each strike plays once on every phone. The memory is in the server's fish log, so a new lead phone carries on where the old one left off. The lead phone sends where every animal is about seven times a second, on a steady timer as well as from its frame loop, so a lead phone that is drawing slowly still keeps the others fresh; each snapshot is stamped with the frame that moved the animals. If the lead phone goes quiet for a few seconds (busy, or briefly hidden), the other phones keep each animal about where it was last seen, still moving in its own way, for up to twenty seconds; only after a longer silence do their own animals carry on. When the news comes back, an animal that has drifted glides back over a second or so and never jumps. `PORT=8124 node tools/two_phones.mjs` (with the dev server on that port) also drops the lead phone's connection and checks that the other phone takes over smoothly; `SMALL=1` draws both pages at half resolution on a machine without a graphics chip.

**Its record.** The profile shows real counts only: meals eaten, strikes from its stem, time holding on, holdfasts tried, visits to its favourite, greetings, tails held and new things inspected. The Personality tab adds a few habit lines read from the same record (`web/src/game/habits.js`), such as "Pip has gone back to the same sea grass 34 times" or "Pip often goes to look at new decorations: 4 so far". A line appears only when the counts behind it are real and large enough, every number in it is a count of things that happened, and the same rules serve fish and octopuses (where they spend their time, how many decorations they have visited).

**Its body and tail.** The seahorse is drawn from a side outline like the lined seahorse's: a crowned head on a bent neck, a long tube snout flared at the tip, a turret eye, a deep ringed trunk with knobs on every ring (some colours have spines and skin filaments), a fluttering dorsal fan and small pectoral fins. The tail is a rig of 52 joints that tapers from the trunk to a fine tip. Swimming it trails out, drifting it hangs in a loose hook, and holding on it wraps round the stem in a helix that tightens as the tail thins, so every turn lies snug against the stem a tail's thickness out and the turns stack down the stem without lying on each other. The tail's cross-section keeps the same side facing in from the flat curl to the wrap, so it never twists over. `OUT=dir node tools/seahorse_look.mjs` shows them close up.

**Holding on without clipping.** It holds on only to something its tail can really go round: a plant's stem or blade, or a thin post, branch or rail on a structure (the anchor, the bridge). Rocks, shells, pots and other smooth shapes are not holdfasts. For each placement of a decoration it looks over the decoration's own voxels, once, for spots where the whole seahorse fits: tail round the stem, body leaning back, and also turned a little either way and flicking its head forward, as it does while it watches. Only a spot where none of that touches a leaf, the decoration or the sand is used. On a thicker post its tail makes fewer, wider turns and it may lean further back. The search runs in the background, a little each frame within a time budget. The grips are kept until a decoration nearby is added, moved, turned or removed. `node tools/seahorse_phase1.mjs grips,perf` checks it. On the last stretch it glides onto the spot and wraps its tail before it counts as holding on. While it holds on, that part of the plant stops swaying and the leaves within reach of its coils are pressed just clear of them in the plant shader; the stem it holds runs through the middle of the coils. Swimming, its floor allows for how far its tail hangs, it curls its tail up rather than drag it through a plant, a rock or the sand, and it eases round plants. Pairs that greet or hold tails meet in open water above the plants. `SEA=1 node tools/clip_check.mjs 4` runs a tank of seahorses and plants and counts any voxel inside a leaf or the sand.

## Seahorse families
Seahorses pair, court, and the male carries the young. All of it is kept in the tank record on the server (`seaFamilies` in `web/src/game/rules.js`), so every caretaker sees the same, and it carries on while every phone is closed.

- **Pairs.** Each seahorse has a sex (from its seed, stored once; older saves get it filled in). The time each adult male and female spend in the tank together adds up, and after about two days they pair (`mate` on both records). Never a parent and its child, never a brother and sister. A pair greets each other first each morning and holds tails far more often than other seahorses; the pair lasts.
- **Courtship and pregnancy.** Now and then (a small chance every six hours, once they have been a pair a day, when no other family is under way and his fortnight's rest is over) a pair courts: for the next few hours they dance again and again, circling close. The male carries the young for six to nine days (`preg`: start, due, mother, brood size). His brood pouch, on his lower belly just above the tail, is part of the seahorse model drawn at its fullest; each of its voxels is marked with how full the pouch must be to show it, so it swells outward a layer at a time as the pregnancy goes on (`broodOf`). He swims, holds on, feeds and greets as always. His profile says how far along he is.
- **Birth.** On the due time, by the server's clock and before any time away is counted against anyone's health, the pregnancy ends and the nursery begins in the same change, so it can only happen once. A phone that is watching sees it: he lets go, straightens, his pouch contracts and the babies come out of it one by one. The journal records it once.
- **The nursery (five days).** The babies grow up in the tank itself. Three of them are tracked one by one (`nursery.cands`: name, sex, traits, blended looks); they swim, hold on and feed as tiny seahorses of their own and grow through the five days. The rest of the brood (ten to fifteen in all) is a light swarm of tiny voxel seahorses in one instanced mesh (`web/src/w3/nursery.js`): they drift near their father at first, explore, dart at food (they never eat it, so nothing in care or the economy changes) and from the second day learn to hold on to plant stems. No new care, equipment or meters; the nursery does not count against the tank's room.
- **Choosing.** After five days the choice comes up by itself, once a session: THEY'RE READY, and the three babies with their names, sex and personality. Any caretaker can choose; it asks for a confirmation; on the server it is one atomic action (`keepBaby`), so if two caretakers choose at once one wins and the other is told someone already chose. The kept baby keeps its identity (the same seahorse on screen), its parents and its birth date, and starts its own growing up from baby; the others swim off. If the tank is full, the choice waits: nothing is ever removed to make room. Its profile says BORN IN YOUR AQUARIUM, with mother, father and birth date.
- **After.** His pouch goes back down over two days; the pair stays together; he cannot carry again for fourteen days. Seahorses no longer lay eggs (the other fish still do).

`node web/src/game/rules.test.mjs` runs the whole lifecycle on simulated time (pairing, relatives, pregnancy length, a birth while every phone is closed, no second birth, the choice racing between two caretakers, a full tank). `OUT=dir node tools/seahorse_family.mjs` plays it in the game: the pouch, the birth, the nursery, the choice and the profile.

## Feeding
Feeding is meant to need no explaining.
- **One tap from Care.** When someone is hungry, the top of Care says so and is a button: "Inky is hungry. Feed Inky a crab ›" takes you straight to the tank with the right food (a crab for the octopus, mysis if only the seahorse is hungry, otherwise flakes). The food list below also opens by itself whenever someone is hungry. Tapping Feed opens or closes it by hand.
- **One group per animal.** Your octopus (a crab each), the seahorse (mysis shrimp, the tiny shrimp seahorses really eat) and the fish (flakes, pellets, treats). Each shows its picture, a fullness bar with "40% full", and Hungry, Peckish or Full. A ♥ marks whose favourite each food is.
- **Full means full.** A full group shows no foods, just "Full for now · can eat again in about 2 hours", and the Feed button says "All full · next meal in about 2 hours" when everyone is.
- **What it earns.** A free food (flakes, mysis, a crab) given to a hungry animal earns 1 🐚, and its chip says "free +1 🐚". Pellets and treats show their price. These are the rules' own thresholds: a meal can be given from 8% hunger and earns from 25%.
- **While feeding.** The strip along the top shows the food (tap it to switch food), who it is for, "Tap the water", three dots for the drops left, and Done. The bell steps aside so nothing is cut off.
- **Shared tanks.** Each group says who fed it last ("Sam fed them 25m ago"), so two caretakers do not feed back to back.

Mysis is free and fills the same shared meal as flakes, paying exactly as flakes do, so it changes nothing in the economy. Only seahorses are cheered by it, and a seahorse always notices it and comes up for it from anywhere in the tank.

## Animal profiles
Tap an animal and open its card: a title and a line about who it is, then four tabs.
- **Overview**: how it is doing, its favourites (food, its holdfast or home, the part of the tank it spends most time in, its companion), how well the tank knows it (New Arrival → Settling In → Familiar → Comfortable → Well Established, from time in the tank, things seen and play), the behaviours seen so far, and the basics (species, scientific name, adult size, life stage, caretaker).
- **Personality**: five bars (curiosity, sociability, boldness, activity, adaptability). They come from what already drives the animal: a fish's traits, an octopus's temperament, a little of its own seed. Then every behaviour it can show, with the ones not seen yet as "?".
- **Stats**: how far it has gone, places visited, holdfasts and snaps for a seahorse, jars, crabs and rearranging for an octopus, and where it spends its time.
- **Journal**: its own story.
Seahorse and octopus behaviours (tail hitching, independent eyes, camouflage, impressions…) are quiet: a line in that animal's journal, no toast and no shells. The lead phone sends a small tally of where each animal went every three minutes (`fishLog`, bounded and rate limited). No new personal data. `node tools/profile_check.mjs OUTDIR` checks it.
