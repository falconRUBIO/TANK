# Progression beyond level 8: a proposal (not implemented)

Nothing here is built. It is a design for review, written so that no level is "bigger numbers and longer waits". Every level adds something to *do* or *see*, and most of it grows out of systems that already exist: personalities, discoveries, eggs and family trees, rare visitors, the journal and the collection book.

## What exists at the end of level 8
- 9 shop fish, 3 rare visitors, 22 decorations, 5 floors, 4 backdrops, 14 behaviour discoveries, eggs with family trees, mortality, one daily wish, 12 long-term tank wishes.
- Tank level thresholds stop at 226 points. Capacity is 28 fish.
- Level 8 has no unlock of its own beyond capacity and the "reach level 8" wish.

## Principles
1. A level should unlock a new *kind of thing to notice or do*, not just a new item.
2. No price inflation and no longer waits. New items cost about what existing ones cost.
3. Builds on lineage first. Family history is the thing only this tank has.
4. Small. Each level is one species or behaviour, one decoration or environment, and one or two discoveries.

## Level 9: "Bloodlines"
- **Breeding becomes visible.** Fish that share a parent show it: siblings keep a faint matching marking and school together. The Family dialog gains a small tree view (three generations).
- **Discoveries:** "Sibling bond" (siblings found swimming together) and "Inherits a trait" (a hatchling shows a parent's personality in its first week).
- **New fish:** Koi (a calm, long-lived goldfish cousin; hatchlings show wider colour variation, so lines can drift over generations).
- **Why:** it rewards the tank for the lineage it has already built, and gives egg-laying a purpose beyond a new fish.

## Level 10: "Moods of the water"
- **New environments:** two backdrops and one floor (for example a kelp forest and a moonlit reef), each with its own ambient behaviour change, such as fish schooling tighter in the kelp or resting longer in the reef.
- **New behaviour:** a gentle current that some plants and fish respond to. Playful fish ride it; shy fish avoid it.
- **Discoveries:** "Rides the current", "Prefers the kelp".
- **Why:** gives players a reason to redesign the tank around how the fish behave, not only how it looks.

## Level 11: "Visitors with a story"
- **Rare visitors get a return pattern.** The same visitor comes back in later weeks, and after a few visits it can be invited to stay (it becomes a resident with a family record of its own).
- **New fish:** one more rare visitor species, plus one that only appears to a tank with a particular decoration.
- **Discoveries:** "Remembers a visitor", "A visitor stays".
- **Why:** turns the strongest retention mechanic (rare visitors) into a relationship instead of a one-off.

## Level 12: "The old tank"
- **Aquarium history becomes an object.** A history page assembled from the journal and memorials: founders, generations, the longest-lived fish, the first friendship, the first death. It is a read-only keepsake the three friends can look at together.
- **New decoration:** a memorial stone fish can be moved beside, which fish that were fond of the deceased visit.
- **Discoveries:** "Visits a memorial".
- **Why:** this is where the six-month tank pays off. Players are looking at their own story.

## What would be measured before building any of this
- Whether players reach level 8 at all, and how long it takes (analytics: tank level distribution, retention at day 30).
- Whether lineage is used (eggs laid, hatchlings, Family dialog opens).
- Whether discoveries are being found by watching (discovery events per active day).
- Whether the daily wish is completed or ignored.

If those numbers are low, levels 9 to 12 should wait and the effort should go into whatever the numbers show is weak.

## What is deliberately not proposed
- Higher prices, longer waits or larger tanks as the main reward.
- Competitive anything between caretakers.
- New purchasable currencies.
